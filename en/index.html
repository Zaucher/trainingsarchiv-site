Why Spurlog

Five things that bug people about fitness apps. And what Spurlog does instead.

  1. I have to pay just to see my own history.

    Your history is never behind a paywall.

    The list of all your activities, every detail view with map, elevation, pace and heart rate profiles, and export are free and will stay free. Once something is free in Spurlog, it never moves behind a purchase.

  2. My tour vanished after the import, and nothing tells me why.

    Every file gets a visible result.

    After an import, every file shows whether it’s new, was already there, or why it doesn’t fit. Your original files are kept unchanged, and if you like, Spurlog backs up your whole archive automatically to a folder of your choice.

  3. Nothing works without a login and a connection.

    No account, no server, no sign-in.

    Everything is stored and calculated on your phone, even in airplane mode. Only the map background comes from the internet. Offline, you see your route as a line.

  4. There are so many features, I can’t find anything.

    Three views, each one tap away.

    Activities, Training Load, Personal Bests. No feed, no ad banners, no pop-ups you didn’t ask for. Every number comes with an explanation of how it’s calculated.

  5. Not another subscription.

    Buy once. Done.

    The full version is a one-time purchase: €9.99 at launch, €14.99 afterwards for €14.99. No subscription, no trial, no automatic renewal. With Family Sharing, your family gets it too.

Training load you don’t need a glossary for.

To be honest

What Spurlog is not made for.

Spurlog doesn’t replace every sports app. It’s the place where your activities live, no matter what you record them with.

Recording
That’s the job of your watch, bike computer or Apple Watch. Spurlog takes over the finished activity.
Feed, kudos and leaderboards
There aren’t any. Nobody sees your training unless you share it yourself.
Route planning and navigation
Spurlog looks back, not ahead.
Training plans and AI coach
Spurlog shows you the numbers and explains where they come from. What you do with them is up to you.
Live tracking
That would need a server. Spurlog doesn’t have one.

What’s next

What’s coming next.

This is what I’m working on next, in this order. I’m deliberately not giving dates, so nothing is promised before it’s ready.

  1. Ski touring in winter

    The “Climbs and descents” card gets more precise.

    • Transitions such as switching skins are detected: how many, how long on average, the longest one and their share of the tour.
    • If your watch records climbs and descents itself, for example Garmin with the “Backcountry Ski” profile, Spurlog uses those sections.
    • Season trend: your climbing rate per tour across the winter, with your heart rate on the climb next to it, plus the season’s descent.
    • Summit stats: how often you’ve been up which summit, when first and last, and your fastest ascent.

  2. Your own entries

    For everything a watch can’t measure.

    • Tap in the conditions of a tour: snow from powder to corn, track, weather, how warm it felt and visibility.
    • In your season summary: your climbing rate per snow type, once there are enough tours.
    • Mark activities as races. Spurlog then shows the total time including every stop and collects your races in a list with distance, elevation gain and time.
    • Your entries are included in backups and exports.

  3. Mountain running

    Analyses that account for gradient and elevation.

    • Climbs as a table: vertical meters per hour, difficulty by the FIETS formula, walking share (when cadence is recorded) and heart rate in the first and second half.
    • Vertical bests: the most elevation gained in 5, 10, 20, 30 and 60 minutes, as a curve next to your pace bests.
    • Pace by gradient: how fast you are on which gradient, also compared with last year.
    • Training load also shows elevation loss, meters climbed per kilometer and time above 1,500, 2,000 and 2,500 m.

  4. Race replay with weather

    A race in hindsight, and what else your file knows.

    • Race replay: every stop with time, duration and altitude, first versus second half uphill and downhill, and how long you were out in the dark.
    • Weather from your watch: temperature, feels-like temperature, humidity and wind, if your watch or Apple Health records them. Straight from the file, no internet needed.
    • Passes and huts in the offline list, so you see the hut’s name instead of just an altitude.
    • Correct climbs and descents by hand when the detection gets it wrong.

FAQ

Quick answers.

When will Spurlog be released?

On the App Store, expected in early December 2026. The Android version comes to Google Play once Google’s mandatory two-week test is complete; the date is not fixed yet. If you like, I’ll let you know by email.

What do I need to use Spurlog?

An iPhone with iOS 18 or later and activities as files or in Apple Health, or an Android device running Android 8 or later and activities as files or in Health Connect (Android 9 or later). The app is available in English and German and uses kilometers or miles depending on your device settings.

Is Spurlog available for Android?

Yes. Before release, Google requires new developers to run a test with at least twelve testers for two weeks. Once that is complete, Spurlog comes to Google Play; the date is not fixed yet. The Android version offers the same analyses as the iPhone version and reads workouts from Health Connect instead of Apple Health. The launch price applies there for four weeks from the Android launch. Testers get the full version for free; you’ll find how to sign up under What’s next.Yes, on Google Play, for devices running Android 8 or later. It offers the same analyses as the iPhone version and reads workouts from Health Connect instead of Apple Health. The full version purchase applies to the store you buy it in.

Do I have to give up my other sports apps?

No. Spurlog doesn’t connect to any service and doesn’t change anything there. You import files or Health workouts and keep using everything else as before.

What happens to my data when I get a new iPhone?

If you use an iPhone backup, the app’s data can be included in it. You can also set up an automatic backup to a folder in Spurlog, for example in iCloud Drive, or export your archive manually as a ZIP. You restore your purchase with your Apple ID.

Does Spurlog read my Health data in the background?

No. Spurlog only reads workouts from Apple Health when you start the import yourself, and it never writes anything to Health. You decide which types of data the app may read. Details are in the privacy policy.

How does Spurlog do its math?

In the open. For training load and race predictions, the app shows which formula and which heart rate values it uses. And because you can export any activity as FIT, GPX or CSV, you can check every number yourself.
